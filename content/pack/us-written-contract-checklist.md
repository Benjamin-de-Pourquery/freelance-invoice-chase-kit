# Written contract checklist (US freelance laws)

Use this before you start work or send a large invoice. It summarizes **written-contract requirements** that may apply when you are hired as an independent contractor in certain US jurisdictions. Thresholds are based on the **value of the engagement**, often including other contracts with the same client in a **120-day** window.

**Last verified:** October 19, 2026 (against the official sources linked below).

**General information only, not legal advice.** Laws change, exceptions apply, and your facts may differ. Confirm requirements on the official site and consult a qualified attorney for your situation.

Published by Glint.

---

## How to use this checklist

1. Note where **you** perform services and where the **hiring party** is located ([STATE_OR_CITY]).
2. If the contract value meets the threshold, confirm you have a **written contract** (or written offer accepted in writing) **before** you rely on payment terms.
3. Compare your draft contract to the **minimum contents** for each law that may apply.
4. Save a copy for your records; note retention periods where the law requires the hiring party to keep the contract.

## New York State: General Business Law Article 44-A (Freelance Isn't Free Act)

**When a written contract may be required:** The hiring party retains a **freelance worker** (as defined in the statute) for services worth **$800 or more**, alone or **aggregated** with other contracts between the same parties in the **immediately preceding 120 days**.

**Official sources:**
- Definitions (freelance worker, hiring party): https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1410
- Written contract contents and retention (6 years by hiring party): https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1412
- Payment timing (30 days if contract silent): https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1411
- Overview and model contract (NYS Dept. of Labor): https://dol.ny.gov/freelance-isnt-free-act

**Minimum contents to include (check each):**
- [ ] Legal name and **mailing address** of hiring party and freelance worker
- [ ] **Itemization** of services, **value** of services, **rate and method** of compensation
- [ ] **Date** payment is due, or the **mechanism** to determine that date
- [ ] **Date** by which the freelance worker must submit a **list of services rendered** (if needed for the hiring party's internal processing deadlines)
- [ ] Each party receives a **copy** of the written contract (physical or electronic)

**Enforcement (general):** Complaints may be filed with the **New York State Attorney General** (see https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/workers-rights).

## New York City: Administrative Code Title 20, Chapter 10 (Freelance Isn't Free Act)

**When a written contract may be required:** Contract between a hiring party and freelance worker with value **$800 or more**, alone or **aggregated** over the **immediately preceding 120 days**. This **$800 threshold applies only to the written-contract requirement**. Timely payment rules under Admin. Code **20-929** apply to covered freelance work **with no dollar threshold**.

**Official sources:**
- Worker rights summary (written contract, timely payment): https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/workers/workersrights/freelancer-workers.page
- FAQ (required terms, 30-day default payment): https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dca/downloads/pdf/workers/FAQs-Freelance.pdf
- Local Law 140 (2016) text: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dca/downloads/pdf/about/Freelance-Law.pdf

**Minimum contents to include (check each):**
- [ ] Name and **mailing address** of hiring party and freelance worker
- [ ] **Itemization** of all services to be provided
- [ ] **Value** of services under the contract
- [ ] **Rate** and **method** of compensation
- [ ] **Payment date** or mechanism to determine the payment date
- [ ] Both parties **keep a copy** of the written contract

**Note:** NYC rules may apply when work is performed in New York City or the hiring party is subject to the city's law. Verify scope on official NYC materials.

## Illinois: Freelance Worker Protection Act (820 ILCS 193)

**When a written contract may be required:** A **contracting entity** retains a **freelance worker** for products or services in Illinois or for an Illinois-based entity, for **$500 or more** in a single contract or **aggregated** over the **immediately preceding 120 days**.

**Official sources:**
- Full act (definitions, payment, contracts): https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=4441&ChapterID=68&Print=True
- Section 15 (written contract): https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/082001930K15.htm
- IDOL summary: https://labor.illinois.gov/laws-rules/legal/freelance-worker-protection-act.html

**Effective date:** Contracts taking effect **after July 1, 2024** (see IDOL summary).

**Minimum contents to include (check each):**
- [ ] Name and **contact information** of contracting entity and freelance worker (including contracting entity **mailing address**)
- [ ] **Itemization** of products and services, **value**, **rate and method** of compensation
- [ ] **Payment date** or mechanism, **no later than 30 days** after products or services are provided (if not specified otherwise as allowed by the statute)
- [ ] **Date** to submit a list of products or services rendered, if required for the entity's internal processing deadlines
- [ ] Contracting entity **furnishes a copy** to the freelance worker; entity must **retain** the contract at least **2 years**

## California: Business and Professions Code §§ 18100-18107 (Freelance Worker Protection Act)

**When a written contract may be required:** A **hiring party** in California retains a **freelance worker** (bona fide independent contractor providing **professional services** as defined in the statute) for **$250 or more**, alone or **aggregated** over the **immediately preceding 120 days**.

**Official sources:**
- BPC Part 5 (§§ 18100-18107): https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&division=7.&part=5.
- Enacting legislation (SB 988, effective **January 1, 2025**): https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB988

**Minimum contents to include (check each):**
- [ ] **Name and mailing address** of each party
- [ ] **Itemized list** of services, **value**, **rate and method** of compensation
- [ ] **Payment date** or mechanism to determine it (if silent, payment **no later than 30 days** after completion of services per § 18102)
- [ ] **Date** to submit a list of services rendered for the hiring party's internal processing deadlines
- [ ] Hiring party provides a **signed copy** to the freelance worker and **retains** the contract at least **4 years**

**Note:** California limits covered work to **professional services** as defined in Labor Code § 2778(b)(2). Confirm your services qualify on the official statute text.

## Before you sign or start work

- [ ] Scope, deliverables, and acceptance criteria are clear
- [ ] Payment amount, currency (**USD**), and invoice procedure are defined
- [ ] Expenses, rush fees, and change orders are addressed
- [ ] Intellectual property and confidentiality terms match your project
- [ ] You saved the signed or agreed written contract in your records
- [ ] You logged the client in `us-unpaid-invoice-tracker.csv` with [STATE_OR_CITY] and possible law

If the hiring party refuses a written contract where one may be required, document your request in email before you begin additional work.
